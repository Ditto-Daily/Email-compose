### Iron doses or upper limit
The upper dose level guidance for supplementing iron is 17mg/day. Ditto provides 14mg (100% recommended daily amount) so you may want to replace your other supplement, unless you know you have low iron or a deficiency, in which case doctors may guide you to take more!

### Isn't iron supposed to be taken every other day for absorption?
You're absolutely right that alternate day iron dosing has some research on it. It actually came from this very small 2 week study investigating alternate vs daily iron dosing with very high doses of iron (120mg) [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29032957/]. 

However, since then, the research has developed with several studies showing no difference between alternate and daily iron dosing. For example, this is the largest randomised controlled trial to date (200 participants) and it found no differences between alternate day versus daily iron in terms of haemoglobin increase or serum ferritin [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892593/].

Similarly a 2025 meta-analysis of 11 studies found that there was no significant difference between daily and alternate iron dosing for hemoglobin, serum iron, ferritin or transferrin saturation, and actually they found daily led to numerically higher haemoglobin! [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12372323/] 

It's worth noting that the mechanistic idea behind original studies testing out alternate iron dosing relates to hepcidin - a hormone that regulates iron absorption and tends to increase after iron supplementation, potentially reducing absorption of subsequent doses. However the hepcidin effect is more pronounced with higher iron doses (e.g. the dose that those studies test is 120mg), whereas DITTO contains 14mg of iron - a moderate dose that's less likely to trigger significant hepcidin elevation so we would not be concerned about this :).
Let me know if you have any questions!

## Questions about the ingredient choices

### What are capsules made from

### What is the % of actives in your saffron?
We source our own saffron extract in DITTO to ensure quality dosing of the actives. Each batch is standardised to 6.56% (4.27% crocin, 2.29% safranal), which is almost double the actives in the branded extracts like Affron (they only need to contain 3.5%!)

### Why does this supplement not contain Vitex agnus castus / chasteberry?
The studies on this ingredient for PMS and PMDD are unfortunately very low quality with high risk of bias. Recent reviewers have concluded that the research available right now on this ingredient is misleading and the majority of studies can't be used as evidence for it's efficacy. Since the evidence wasn't solid enough, we decided not to include it in DITTO. We only wanted ingredients where we're confident the research actually backs them up!

### Why haven't you used affron branded saffron?
We chose not to use Affron® for Ditto for quite a few reasons! The first is because it hasn't been specifically studied in PMS or PMDD research. We wanted to stick with the saffron extract that has clinical evidence in supporting the populations we're here to help.

Secondly, the Affron® branded extract has only been studied since 2017 and has just 11 trials so far (some of these not measuring mood). Standard saffron extract, on the other hand, has a much longer research history going back to the early 2000s, with many hundreds of studies showing benefits. From what we can see from the totality of the research, there isn't strong evidence that Affron is better - especially not for what we're trying to achieve.

And finally, one thing you'll often read is that Affron is standardised (meaning consistent amount of actives in each batch - which is great). The good news is that we standardise our saffron extract too! We test every single batch to make sure it's consistent with the amount of active ingredients it provides. Affron is standardised to contain at least 3.5% of the combined bioactive compounds, and our saffron extract in DITTO exceeds this with 6.56% (4.27% crocin, 2.29% safranal), which is very high quality.

I thought you might find these meta-analyses interesting:

- A 46-study meta-analysis (only 4 used Affron) – the other studies showed positive effects across the board: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38424688/

- PMS study: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S187638201530010X

### Why no calcium?
We did not include calcium, because while some evidence suggests benefit in PMS, there is actually growing research raising concerns about potential cardiovascular risks specifically from calcium when it's taken from supplements.

We did not want to introduce this risk, and since there are so many other powerful nutrients that target the same symptoms, upon weighing everything up we decided not to include this one in particular.
